The Math: Power & Decibels

SNR measures the ratio of signal power to noise power.

SNR = 10 × log₁₀(Psignal / Pnoise) dB
What is Power?

Power = average of squared values

P = mean(x²)
Why Decibels?

Ratios can be huge (10x, 100x, 1000x). dB compresses the scale.

Quick Reference

🐛 The Critical Bug

Here's code found in production systems. Can you spot the bug?

def calculate_snr(audio, noise):
    signal_power = mean(audio ** 2)  # ← THE BUG
    noise_power = mean(noise ** 2)
    return 10 * log10(signal_power / noise_power)
⚠️ The bug: audio contains BOTH signal AND noise!

Wrong: (Signal + Noise) / Noise = 1 + Signal/Noise
Correct: Signal / Noise

This bug always overestimates SNR.

Real-World Estimation

In practice, you only have the mixed audio. How do you estimate SNR?

💡 The Key Insight

Speech has pauses. Music has quiet moments. During silence, you only hear noise!

■ Red = noise only ■ Blue = signal + noise
The Percentile Method
  1. Split audio into short frames (25ms each)
  2. Calculate energy of each frame
  3. Find frames with lowest energy (bottom 10%)
  4. These are your "noise frames"
  5. Estimate noise power from these frames
⚠️ Limitation: This fails for continuous signals with no pauses!

Key Takeaways

→ SNR = ratio of signal power to noise power (in dB)

→ Every +10 dB = 10× more signal power

→ Common bug: using (S+N)/N instead of S/N

→ Estimate noise from quiet frames (percentile method)
