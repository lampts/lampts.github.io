Logprobs Lab

Teaching LLMs to Know What They Don't Know

When an LLM generates text, it assigns a probability to every possible next word. These probabilities are the key to building AI systems that can tell you when they're uncertain.

Consider this

An LLM says "The capital of France is Paris" and also says "The best restaurant in Paris is Le Petit Cler".

Both arrive as plain text. Should you trust them equally?

Which statement should you trust more?

Key Insight

Without access to the model's internal confidence, both statements look equally trustworthy. Logprobs expose this hidden uncertainty—they tell you how confident the model was when generating each token.

What is a Logprob?

A log probability (logprob) is the natural logarithm of a probability. Instead of saying "90% confident", the model reports -0.105.

logprob = ln(probability)

or equivalently

probability = elogprob

Why logarithms?

Probabilities can be tiny (0.0000001). Logarithms compress this range and make the math easier.

Use Case: Ambiguity Detection

What if the model is 52% spam, 48% not spam? That's a coin flip, not a classification.

Key Insight

It's not just the top choice's confidence. It's the margin between top two choices.

The Pattern

grounding = conf_with_context - conf_without

High score → Context helped (good retrieval)

Low score → Wrong document or hallucinating

Negative → Context confused the model

Use Case: Hallucination Detection

LLMs hallucinate. But token-level logprobs often reveal the lie.

The Pattern

"Confident structure, uncertain content" — confident on "The CEO of X is" but uncertain on the actual name.

Example 1: Known entity (click tokens)

The CEO of Anthropic is Dario Amodei

✓ All tokens have reasonable confidence

Example 2: Potential hallucination

The CEO of Acme Corp is John Smith

⚠ Entity tokens have low confidence — likely hallucination

Detection Rule

if is_entity(token) and logprob < -1.5: flag()
