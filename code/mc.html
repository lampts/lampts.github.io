Module 0 / 5 — The Problem

The Question Everyone Asks

You're 30 years old. You have $50,000 saved. You invest $500 every month. The stock market averages about 7% per year. Will you have enough to retire at 65?

❌ Wrong approach: Plug 7% into a compound interest calculator and get one number. That number is a lie — the market doesn't return 7% every year.

✅ Right approach: Some years are up 20%. Others are down 15%. The sequence matters. A crash at 60 hurts far more than a crash at 32. You need to simulate the randomness.

The Monte Carlo Setup
Ω All possible sequences of 35 annual returns
f Compound portfolio forward: apply returns, add contributions each year
xᵢ One random sequence: [+12%, -8%, +22%, ...] for 35 years
N 1,000 simulated lifetimes
Ê Distribution of final portfolio values → P(enough to retire)
Why Not Just Use 7%?

Because the deterministic answer hides a critical truth: the spread of outcomes. Two people with identical savings plans can end up $1M apart because of when crashes happen.

Deterministic (7% flat)
—
One number. No uncertainty.
Monte Carlo (7% avg, 15% std)
—
Range of 1,000 futures.

THE MARKET AVERAGES 7%. IT NEVER RETURNS 7%. THAT GAP IS EVERYTHING.

Module 3 / 5 — The Distribution of Outcomes

Answering Practical Questions

"What's the typical outcome? What's the probability of reaching $800K?"

❌ Wrong: Reporting just the average. Averages hide the tails that ruin or make you.

✅ Right: Report the full distribution. The median (typical), the 10th percentile (bad luck), the 90th percentile (good luck), and the probability of hitting your target.

Key Insight
P(goal) = count(final ≥ goal) / N

MC converts impossible probability calculations into simple counting. "What fraction of my 1,000 simulated lifetimes hit the target?" That fraction IS the probability.

Module 4 / 5 — Sensitivity Analysis

Which Lever Matters Most?

"Instead of guessing, we simulate."

❌ Wrong: Optimizing one variable in isolation. "If I just get better returns..."

✅ Right: MC lets you run controlled experiments on your future. Change one variable, hold others fixed, compare distributions. Find the lever with the highest marginal impact.

Key Insight
∂P(goal) / ∂lever — which lever moves the needle most?

You can only control: how much you save, how long you work, and your asset allocation (which affects both mean and volatility). MC tells you which matters.

Module 5 / 5 — Synthesis

The Complete Machine

"Instead of guessing, we simulate."

The Algorithm in 6 Lines
for sim in 1..N:
    balance = starting_savings
    for year in 1..35:
        r = random_normal(mean=0.07, std=0.15)
        balance = balance * (1 + r) + annual_contribution
    record(balance)
report: median, percentiles, P(balance ≥ goal)

That's it. The entire retirement Monte Carlo in 6 lines. The power isn't in complexity — it's in running it 1,000 times and reading the distribution.

What MC Told Us
→ Typical outcome (median): ~$1.2M — but this is NOT guaranteed
→ P(≥ $800K) ≈ 75% — three in four futures work out
→ Worst cases: ~$400K — one in four futures underdeliver
→ Monthly contribution has more reliable impact than chasing returns
→ More years compounds both wealth AND uncertainty
